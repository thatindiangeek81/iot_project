03Pipelines — how, when, why

Seven paths through the same hardware. Pipelines 1, 2 and 7 never leave the S3.

1

Always listening

Edge< 0.5 s
When
Always, even while music is playing.
How
INMP441→Audio Front-End→WakeNet “Jarvis”→chime + duck music
Why
Nothing leaves the board until the wake word, so it's private, there's no 24/7 upload (unlike BlueDot), and it works offline.
2

Local command

Edge< 0.5 s
When
What you say is on your MultiNet list: “volume up”, “pause”, “next”, “light on”.
How
MultiNet match→Intent Router→Local Actions→GPIO / player→chime
Why
Instant, free, works with Wi-Fi down. Limit: fixed English phrases only (up to 300).
3

Ask anything

EdgeFogCloud~2–4 s
When
Nothing on the list matched: open questions, chat.
How
buffered speech→WebSocket→faster-whisper→Orchestrator→Groq LLM→Piper TTS→Audio Player
Why
Open-ended language needs a real LLM. Your TinyStories test showed the S3 can't do it.
Tip: send the reply to Piper sentence by sentence so it starts talking before the full answer arrives.
4

Web search

EdgeFogCloud~3–5 s
When
The question needs live info: news, scores, prices, weather.
How
same as 3→Groq asks for a search→Orchestrator→Web Search→one-line summary→Piper TTS→Audio Player
Why
The LLM's knowledge is frozen. Search runs upstream; the speaker only narrates the answer.
5

Play music

EdgeFogCloud~3–5 s
When
“Play song / artist / station”.
How
buffered speech→faster-whisper→Groq picks “play music”→Orchestrator finds stream→URL to S3→Audio Player streams
Why
Song names are open vocabulary, which MultiNet can't handle. Playback still runs on the edge, so pause, volume and next stay instant (pipeline 2).
Sources: your MP3s on the laptop, or internet radio. The S3 has no Bluetooth Classic, so there's no A2DP; music comes over Wi-Fi.
6

Telegram remote

CloudEdge~1 s
When
You message the bot from anywhere.
How
Telegram Bot API→S3 long-poll→Intent Router→Local Actions→reply “Light on ✓”
Why
One router for voice and text. Switching things stays on the edge.
Free text (not a known command) goes Router → Orchestrator → Groq and comes back as a text reply, with no speech involved.
7

Offline fallback

Edgeinstant
When
Wi-Fi, the laptop or Groq can't be reached.
How
escalation times out→Intent Router→offline prompt (SAM / stored clip)→local commands keep working
Why
Graceful degradation. It's still a useful speaker, not a dead one.

  • Both I2S ports run at once (mic on I2S0, amp on I2S1), so it can hear you while it talks: barge-in now, echo cancellation later. BlueDot had to stop the mic to play.
  • Split the cores: speech AI on one, Wi-Fi + playback on the other.
  • Flash: add a model partition for the ESP-SR models and grow the app partition. Your current 1 MB factory app is too small; 16 MB has room.
  • PSRAM (8 MB) holds the models, the speech buffer and the stream buffers.
  • INMP441 sends 24-bit audio in 32-bit slots. Convert to 16-bit, 16 kHz before ESP-SR.
  • Spare GPIOs for relays and sensors: avoid 0, 3, 45, 46 (strapping), 19–20 (USB), 26–37 (flash/PSRAM) and 43–44 (UART). Use a relay module, never a bare coil.
  • No Bluetooth Classic on the S3, so no A2DP. Upside: the Wi-Fi/BT packet-loss problem from the WROOM build disappears.
  • Power: one USB-C 5 V supply, common ground, amp on 5 V.

06Build order

Each step works on its own before the next one starts.

  1. Mic on the S3: record, then play it back through the amp (loopback test).Edge
  2. Wake word: esp-skainet example, “Jarvis” model, chime on detect.Edge
  3. MultiNet → Local Actions: volume, pause, one relay.Edge
  4. Fog link: WebSocket to the laptop; port your faster-whisper → Groq → Piper relay.FogCloud
  5. Music: stream player, “play music” tool, ducking.EdgeFog
  6. Web search tool + Telegram bot.CloudEdge
  7. Phase 2: move the speech-to-text, LLM and text-to-speech calls onto the S3, and drop the laptop.EdgeCloud
Latency figures are rough targets, not measurements. “Jarvis” is a stock ESP-SR wake word model trained on synthetic speech, so test it in your room before relying on it.
